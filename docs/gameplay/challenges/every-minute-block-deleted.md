# Minecraft Game Mode: Every Minute a Random Block is Deleted

## Core Mechanics
Every 60 seconds, the plugin selects a random block **type** and permanently deletes it from the entire world. Every instance of that type vanishes from loaded chunks and stops generating in new ones. Deletion is final: once a type is gone it never returns, and a type already deleted cannot be picked again ("netherrack like cuz it's been deleted once, it won't get deleted again"). The game tends to pick types that are loaded/nearby, so loading more biomes loads more candidate types and spreads the risk. Items already in your inventory are kept even after their block type is deleted. The only exceptions are bedrock and portal frames, since otherwise the game would be nearly impossible. Blocks are deleted, not broken, so chest contents that get deleted are simply lost. If a region runs out of valid blocks, the timer can roll a normally-absent type (e.g. picking end stone while in the Nether), which is rare but never zero.

## Impact on Gameplay
- **Permanent scarcity:** Critical types (stone, iron ore, netherrack) can vanish with no recovery, so progress is a race against the clock.
- **Death spiral:** The slower you go, the more important blocks disappear, making everything even slower.
- **Terrain collapse:** Deleting grass, then dirt and deep slate, leaves a floating, "surface of Mars" landscape with no walkable ground.
- **Lethal Nether:** Losing netherrack/crimson/warp stems strands players on islands of nothing over lava.
- **Severe lag:** Mass deletions across loaded chunks (and a "kelp singularity" ocean) tank server performance.

## Notable Strategies
- **Stock redundancy:** Hoard saplings, dirt, gravel, and spare blocks; keep beehives and leaves loaded as "harmless" candidates to soak rolls.
- **Color hedging:** Craft a backup bed in a different color so a deleted bed color doesn't end the run.
- **Split up:** Spread players across biomes so deletions hit non-essential types instead of all netherrack at once.
- **Bridge with deleted blocks:** Build using types already deleted (now safe) rather than the terrain you stand on.
- **Carry lava for portals:** Stockpile enough lava to rebuild a Nether portal on demand.

## Endgame Adaptation
A full clear required a reset. Attempt one soft-locked when the entire surface and Nether terrain were deleted with no reachable fortress. Attempt two used a faster fortress rush. A soul-sand fortress yielded an air-floating blaze spawner (blazes still spawned mid-air). Ender pearls came from a warped forest where endermen fell to their deaths and "rained" pearls into the team's hands. With overworld terrain gone, the stronghold was easy to spot as an exposed chasm. In the End, players rotated in one at a time so deaths abroad granted more block-rolls, then rushed the dragon while it perched.

## Memorable Moments
- Cyber repeatedly died standing on doomed blocks, including a crimson stem he had just been warned about.
- Endermen "rained" into a lake and dropped six ender pearls at once.
- Leaving the End reloaded the overworld and deleted everything, including the loot chest with all their gear.
- Fish, the last player alive at one heart surrounded by deleted "magic," killed the dragon and won the run.
