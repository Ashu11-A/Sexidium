# Minecraft Game Mode: Every Swing Hits Everything

## Core Mechanics
Every time the player swings their hand, the swing hits **every mob in render distance at once** — below ground, above ground, and even entities like boats. It is a full-render-distance cleave, not just a nearby AoE. Critically, Minecraft does not reward spam attacks: the **first** hit lands full damage, every hit after it does almost nothing. So out of the thousands of mobs struck, only **one** takes full damage, and that full hit goes to a **random** mob. Mining works the same way — breaking one block (a log, stone, obsidian) drains the **full durability** of the tool because the swing also strikes every mob around, so tools and weapons "break before I can use them." A second rule softens this: whenever any mob dies, its **drops and XP teleport straight to the player**, and items even arrive pre-cooked once the sword has Fire Aspect.

## Impact on Gameplay
- **Saturation drains instantly:** breaking three logs cost four hunger bars; the player can only mine "two blocks with zero hunger before I take damage."
- **No durable gear by hand:** a diamond pickaxe lasts ~3 swings, and two obsidian blocks cost half its durability, making normal progression nearly impossible.
- **Swinging is suicide in hostile zones:** one punch aggros all piglins, zombified piglins, or Endermen at once. Endermen clipping through walls became the player's single greatest cause of death.
- **Right-click items conflict:** a shield in the hotbar couldn't be raised because every right-click item fired its own effect instead.
- **Hostile mobs are slow to kill:** they have over twice the HP of passive mobs, and minimum damage makes grinding them painfully slow.

## Notable Strategies
- **TNT for everything:** killing creepers for gunpowder, then using TNT to mine cobblestone, sand, trees, and to blow up villager workstations to free up jobs.
- **The Fire Aspect + Mending sword:** burns every mob in one swing while self-repairing on each kill — chat-suggested ("Fire Aspect, please notice me"), grinded via librarian/villager trades. Later refined by **anvil-combining** Sharpness, Looting, Fire Aspect, Unbreaking, and Mending into "the cleanser."
- **Render-distance lowering** to avoid hitting mobs, and the **F3+1 pie chart trick** to count chunks to a blaze spawner.
- **Iron golem farming** for iron; **bed/boat traps**; punching the floor so swing knockback prevents mobs from landing hits.

## Endgame Adaptation
In the **Nether**, the rule meant avoiding all swinging until a blaze spawner was located via the pie chart trick. Even at zero strength the Fire Aspect sword's base damage beat the bare hand, so blazes (immune to fire) still died, and striders were knocked off ledges. In the **End**, the player couldn't punch — any swing aggros every Enderman — so the plan was bow-shooting the crystals, then **bed-bombing** the dragon from inside the bedrock pillars, with infinite-water sources for Enderman safety. An early attempt at one-cycling the crystals by swinging caused a mass Enderman aggro and death.

## Memorable Moments
- Killing an iron golem inside a house through a window, which wiped out all the nearby villagers and yielded a bow with 55 arrows.
- Saving a cow stranded in the ocean by escorting it to land ("We're doing God's work, dude").
- A villager who refused to take a job, having "learned" that getting employed meant getting hit again when the lectern was broken.
- Finding multiple totems in a woodland mansion that saved the player from death twice.
- A "cavey end" with literal caves, infinite ender pearls spawning as Endermen kept dying and respawning.
- The roughly 27-hour challenge finally ending with the dragon killed by exploding beds.
