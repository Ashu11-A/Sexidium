# Minecraft Game Mode: I GROW Every Minute

## Core Mechanics
Both players' physical size (scale/hitbox) increases once every minute, and the growth **never stops** — "the growing has no limit." There is no maximum cap; the player keeps getting taller indefinitely until they become physically unable to fit in the world. Growing also has stat side effects established in-video: as they grow, their **strength is higher** (mining faster, eventually breaking more than one block at a time, even breaking **bedrock** when huge), and they take **reduced damage** ("we don't take full damage"). Combat scales too — at large sizes they expect to one-shot mobs and the Ender Dragon. The central tension is that the ever-growing hitbox makes the player too big for caves, portals, and tight spaces, with suffocation as the constant death threat.

## Impact on Gameplay
- **Constant suffocation risk:** Being underground, in caves, or in any enclosed space means they will outgrow it within minutes and suffocate. "We got to go fast so we don't suffocate."
- **Faster mining as a bonus:** Higher strength lets them mine faster and eventually break multiple blocks at once, later even breaking bedrock.
- **Reduced incoming damage:** Mobs hit for less; creepers and skeletons look tiny relative to them, though skeletons and lava can still chip them down.
- **Portals become impossible to enter:** A normal Nether portal is far too small. They must build oversized portals and use trap doors to slip through the gap.
- **The Nether shrinks around them:** Eventually they grow "too big for the Nether," struggling to grab obsidian and risking suffocation in the low ceiling.

## Notable Strategies
- **Trap-door portals:** Place trap doors on an oversized portal frame so their giant hitbox can swim/slip through.
- **Chest-shrinking items:** Dropped items were too big to enter portals, so they put items in a chest and broke it to shrink the dropped stacks small enough to pass through.
- **Nether-roof travel:** Met on the Nether roof because at giant size they could break bedrock to move freely.
- **Death-relay obsidian run:** One player had the other kill him to respawn small in the Overworld, gather obsidian/diamonds, and shuttle them back through a loaded portal.
- **Ender-pearl entry to the End:** Too big for the End portal, they tried boats, swimming head-first, and lobbing pearls into the air to teleport into the tiny portal crevice.

## Endgame Adaptation
They completed a full playthrough. Growth turned every gateway into a puzzle: oversized Nether portal plus trap doors to enter, the death-relay to escape a Nether that had become too cramped, and the End portal solved only by ender-pearling into the small frame after boat and swimming attempts failed. By the End, their size made them so strong they **killed the Ender Dragon in roughly 3 seconds** — the only real challenge was fitting through the portal, not the fight.

## Memorable Moments
- Mining diamonds underground while shrinking-looking creepers seemed tiny, then realizing they barely take damage.
- The "Hulk" dig straight up out of a cave, accidentally finding diamonds on the way.
- Discovering bedrock could be broken at giant size, enabling Nether-roof travel.
- The chest-break trick to shrink items so they'd fit through a portal.
- One player getting killed on purpose to respawn small and relay obsidian back.
- One-shotting each other by accident due to overwhelming strength.
- Killing the Ender Dragon in about 3 seconds to win.
