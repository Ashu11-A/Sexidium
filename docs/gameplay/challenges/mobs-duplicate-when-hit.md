# Minecraft Game Mode: Mobs Duplicate When Hit

## Core Mechanics
When any mob takes damage **caused by a player**, it is instantly duplicated into an exact copy. As the creator stresses, this is not just spawning a similar creature, it is "an exact duplicate. The very soul of the creature is recreated." A duplicate keeps its precise state: villagers copy with the same job, trades and stats ("it duplicates them exactly the same"), and baby animals duplicate as babies ("the baby duplicates, too"). Only player-caused damage triggers it. Late in the run he confirms outright: "mobs only duplicate when they take damage caused by a player. And as you saw, beds don't count as player damage." Each separate hit spawns more copies, so repeatedly punching one mob multiplies it fast (one parrot became two, then "three. Four, five, six... like eight birds").

## Impact on Gameplay
- **Combat becomes near-suicidal:** Hitting one enemy turns it into an army ("you're not fighting one dragon, you're fighting an army"), so it plays like a forced pacifist run.
- **Friendly mobs are an infinite resource:** Punching an iron golem gives "infinite golem" and a shower of iron; cows, fish and other passives multiply for endless drops.
- **Self-inflicted damage stacks up:** Duplicated armed mobs each deal their own damage, so even pets duped on leads "can actually kill me cuz their stuff deals damage now."
- **Crowd chaos:** Areas clog instantly ("there's like a billion cows now"), making simple tasks like testing a villager's job difficult.

## Notable Strategies
- **Punch passives for loot:** Duplicate golems for iron, fish and cows for food, rather than mining or grinding.
- **Build a pet army:** Tame wolves and cats, then duplicate them with a "love tap" so they kill mobs the player can't safely hit.
- **Trade, don't kill:** Trade villagers for ender pearls instead of killing endermen, dodging duplication entirely.
- **Item-holding dupe attempts:** He tries to dupe valuables via mobs that hold items (endermen, foxes, a copper golem holding a diamond), hoping for "infinite diamonds."

## Endgame Adaptation
The Nether and End are both reached. In the Nether he brings only a few wolves to kill blazes, refreshing the pack by punching to make more ("if I need more, I can always give them a love tap"). In the End the core problem peaks: hitting the dragon or its crystals risks spawning fakes. He deduces only the **perching** dragon is real and uses bed explosions (which don't duplicate) to damage it. His first attempt fails amid duplicated dragons "yeeted into the sky"; he then practices one-cycling off-stream and wins the next day on a perch-timed bed strat.

## Memorable Moments
- Accidentally punching his parrot over and over, multiplying it into a flock as parrots "spawn in front of me" while he walks.
- "Infinite golem" forming "a perfect circle," leaving him "showered in stuff."
- Duping pets via leads, then the duplicated armed mobs nearly killing him.
- Obsessing over "Little Jeb/Jeep," a beloved wolf he frantically keeps alive through Nether, ocean and End.
- A duped villager appearing to interact with its own copy ("Are you doing it with yourself?").
- First End run collapsing into a swarm of fake dragons, then finally one-cycling the perching dragon for his first-ever dragon win.
